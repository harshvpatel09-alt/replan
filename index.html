Everything a planner should have done years ago

You give Replan your term once. It handles the arithmetic of fitting the work into the hours you actually have.

A schedule that builds itself

Enter your classes, assignments and test dates once. Replan fills the gaps in your week with study blocks sized to the work in front of you.

It reshuffles when you slip

Skipped Tuesday night? The remaining work spreads across the days you have left — before the deadline, not after it. No guilt, no manual rebuild.

Every AP exam date, built in

All 43 courses from the 2026–27 catalog with their official May 2027 slots, delivery modes and portfolio deadlines. Pick yours and Replan counts down and flags conflicts.

The timer is already inside

Start a block and the Pomodoro timer runs against that exact task. Breaks are part of the plan rather than something you improvise.

It learns your real pace

Check off what you finish. If lab writeups always run long, future blocks get longer — the plan stops being optimistic about you.

Crunch weeks, flagged early

When three tests land in the same week, you find out in advance — with the work already pulled forward into the quiet days before it.

Falling behind stops being a crisis

Every other planner hands you a plan and blames you for breaking it. Replan treats a missed block as new information and rebuilds around it, in the time you have left.

Three steps, then you stop thinking about it

  1. Step 1

    Describe your term

    Classes, assignment due dates, test dates, and the hours you are genuinely free. Ten minutes at the start of the semester.

  2. Step 2

    Open it each morning

    Today’s blocks are already there, sized and ordered. Start the timer on the first one and work through the list.

  3. Step 3

    Miss one anyway

    Practice runs late, you get sick, you just do not feel like it. The plan absorbs it overnight and tomorrow is ready.
